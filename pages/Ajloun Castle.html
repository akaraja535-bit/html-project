Ajloun Castle

Ajloun Castle (Qal'at Ar-Rabad) is a 12th-century Muslim fortress situated in the highlands of northern Jordan. It was built by Izz ad-Din Usama, a general of Saladin, in 1184 AD.

It was strategically built to guard the trade routes between Damascus and Egypt, and it played a vital role in the defense against the Crusaders.

Key Highlights:

  1. Medieval Military Architecture: The castle is a prime example of Islamic fortification, featuring moats, drawbridges, and massive towers.
  2. Panoramic Vistas: From the ramparts, visitors can see across the Jordan Valley, the Sea of Galilee, and the West Bank.
  3. The Museum: The castle contains a small museum displaying artifacts from the site.
  4. Ajloun Forest Reserve: The surrounding area is covered in lush green hills and oak forests, offering excellent trails for hiking and nature observation.
